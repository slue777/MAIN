# AI Bubble Catalysts: Expert Views on What Could End the AI Boom (2025–2026)

Scope note: This file catalogs expert-identified trigger scenarios for an AI bust as of late 2025 through 2026, with named proponents, reasoning, dates, figures, and probabilities. It pays special attention to where a "confidence/sentiment shock" or figurehead (Jensen Huang) departure fits relative to fundamental triggers, to feed a downstream scenario analysis of whether Huang's resignation could itself be a catalyst. Dates are noted throughout because views evolved across 2025–2026. Many sources could only be reached via search-engine summaries (direct page fetch was blocked by the session's network settings), so exact figures flagged as "per secondary source" should be verified against primaries where load-bearing.

## Q1. What catalysts do experts most frequently cite for an AI bust? (and who makes each argument)

### Takeaway
Experts cluster around a handful of fundamental triggers: (1) AI revenue/ROI failing to catch up with capex, valuations, and debt; (2) hyperscaler capex retrenchment; (3) an interest-rate/credit shock hitting debt-financed buildouts; (4) a credit event in data-center/circular financing; (5) cheaper Chinese models (DeepSeek-style) collapsing the "frontier needs the most expensive hardware" assumption; (6) power/energy bottlenecks; and (7) extreme market concentration making the index fragile to any of the above. Most stress that no single lever (e.g. rates alone) is decisive; the fastest-cited path is disappointing monetization.

### Cited Findings — Monetization / ROI failing to catch up
- An AI investment bubble would "most likely burst if commercial returns fail to catch up with the enormous spending, optimistic valuations and growing debt now supporting the sector" — widely framed as the central fundamental trigger — [itechguides via WebSearch](https://www.itechguides.com/what-could-cause-an-ai-investment-bubble-to-burst/); [The Week](https://theweek.com/uk/tech/can-soaring-ai-avoid-a-crash-landing)
- MIT NANDA "GenAI Divide" report (published ~July–Aug 2025): 95% of organizations report **no measurable business return** from generative-AI initiatives; study spanned six months, cited $30–40bn of enterprise investment, 300+ programs, 350 interviews, 150+ senior-leader survey — [Fortune, Aug 21 2025](https://dc.fortune.com/2025/08/21/an-mit-report-that-95-of-ai-pilots-fail-spooked-investors-but-the-reason-why-those-pilots-failed-is-what-should-make-the-c-suite-anxious); [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/the-ai-report-thats-spooking-wall-street-2000645518)
- The MIT report coincided with an Aug 2025 tech selloff: Nvidia fell ~3.5% in a day, Palantir dropped 9.4%; Sam Altman's comment that there is "an AI bubble" in venture-backed startups was cited alongside it — [inkl/MarketBeat](https://www.inkl.com/news/why-mit-study-on-enterprise-market-is-pressuring-ai-stocks)
- Gavekal Research argued "one of the clearest indicators of the AI bubble's burst is the discrepancy between tech companies' promises and their performance"; Gavekal said the bubble "has started to implode" — [StreetInsider](https://www.streetinsider.com/AI/AI+bubble+has+started+to+implode%3A+Gavekal+Research/23549935.html)
- Forrester Research (bearish forecast, late Oct 2025) predicted "a reckoning for corporate AI spending" — [Yahoo Finance/Fortune](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/one-ai-bubble-already-burst-112800354.html)

### Cited Findings — Interest rates / monetary policy
- Ruchir Sharma argues monetary policy decides the outcome: higher rates — e.g. a 10-year Treasury yield above 5% — could dry up cheap capital and end the boom; he expects prices to go "parabolic" before the pop and described eventual damage as "severe but not permanent" — [Yahoo Finance/Fortune](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/one-ai-bubble-already-burst-112800354.html)
- Ray Dalio's central thesis is debt + rates: the large amount of debt raised to fund AI investment is the main risk; as rates climb, that debt becomes harder to carry — [Stocktwits](https://stocktwits.com/news-articles/markets/equity/ray-dalio-reportedly-warns-ai-bubble-is-close-to-bursting-as-rising-rates-test-debt-fueled-spending/cZDvIxzRBmQ)
- Caveat: one source notes traditional macro triggers may "matter less now" because markets are larger and less tied to any single lever such as interest rates — [itechguides](https://www.itechguides.com/what-could-cause-an-ai-investment-bubble-to-burst/)

### Cited Findings — Credit event / circular ("ouroboros") financing in data centers
- IMF reportedly highlighted **14 circular financing arrangements** across the broader GPU ecosystem — [per secondary summaries](https://www.futuriom.com/articles/news/tearing-apart-oracles-openai-deals/2025/09)
- The loop described by analysts: SoftBank invests in OpenAI → OpenAI commits to Oracle → Oracle borrows to build data centers → centers buy Nvidia chips → Nvidia invests back into OpenAI — [Quartz](https://qz.com/ai-circular-economy-deals-nvidia-synopsys-openai-oracle); [Techstrong](https://techstrong.ai/features/what-a-tangled-web-openai-is-becoming-too-interconnected-to-fail/)
- Oracle issued ~$18bn in corporate bonds for OpenAI-related data-center capacity; KeyBanc forecast Oracle may need to borrow up to ~$100bn over four years; Oracle reportedly has ~$288bn of not-yet-started data-center leases (15–19 yr terms) — [Invezz](https://invezz.com/news/2025/12/16/yes-the-ai-boom-has-a-balance-sheet-problem/); [Invezz Nov 28 2025](https://invezz.com/news/2025/11/28/openai-linked-borrowing-approaches-100b-as-data-centre-funding-expands/)
- WSJ (as cited) reported Oracle-related debt "began clogging bank balance sheets," and that Nvidia was discussing a guarantee supporting ~$250bn in financing for an OpenAI data-center development in Ohio; Oracle stock fell ~30% in Q3 on delivery worries — [Invezz](https://invezz.com/news/2025/12/16/yes-the-ai-boom-has-a-balance-sheet-problem/)
- AI data-center/project-finance issuance ~$125bn in 2025, up from ~$15bn a year earlier; Morgan Stanley estimate of a ~$1.5T financing gap to be filled by credit markets (per secondary compilation) — [io-fund](https://io-fund.com/ai-stocks/ai-debt-nears-500-billion-nvidia-oracle-coreweave)
- No source found reporting an actual default/credit event yet (gap).

### Cited Findings — Chinese competition / cheaper models (DeepSeek)
- See Q6 for the Jan 2025 DeepSeek episode. The structural catalyst: DeepSeek-V3 reportedly trained on Nvidia's lower-capability H800 chips for <$6M (DeepSeek's own claim), challenging the assumption that frontier AI needs the most expensive hardware and the largest capex — [Fortune, Jan 28 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/01/28/deepseek-what-it-means-for-tech-stocks-ai-startups-and-everything-in-between/); [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2025/1/28/why-chinas-ai-startup-deepseek-is-sending-shockwaves-through-global-tech)

### Cited Findings — Power/energy bottleneck
- Power availability described as "the defining constraint, overshadowing capital, land, and connectivity" (Bloom Energy) — [Data Center Frontier](https://www.datacenterfrontier.com/energy/article/55363740/the-gigawatt-bottleneck-power-constraints-define-ai-data-center-growth)
- Gartner: 40% of data centers could face power-availability constraints by 2027; ERCOT raised its 2030 data-center demand projection from 29 GW to 77 GW in one planning cycle; transformers now have 4–5 year lead times — [Data Center Frontier](https://www.datacenterfrontier.com/energy/article/55363740/the-gigawatt-bottleneck-power-constraints-define-ai-data-center-growth); [ITPro](https://www.itpro.com/infrastructure/data-centres/ai-data-center-growth-stymied-by-power-constraints)

### Cited Findings — Overcapacity / demand air-pocket
- Overcapacity risk if consumer demand is lower than expected; circular financing (big tech invests in AI startups that then buy big tech's products) inflates apparent demand — [itechguides](https://www.itechguides.com/what-could-cause-an-ai-investment-bubble-to-burst/)
- BIS: the five largest hyperscalers set to spend >$1 trillion on AI capex in 2025–2026, with commitments already outpacing earnings and free cash flow — [per WebSearch summary of BIS warning](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/one-ai-bubble-already-burst-112800354.html)

### Inferences
- The most-cited "fastest" trigger is a monetization/ROI disappointment that punctures the narrative; rates and credit are seen as accelerants that convert a narrative wobble into forced selling (debt must be refinanced at higher cost, circular deals unwind).
- Several catalysts are interlinked: a ROI disappointment could trigger hyperscaler capex cuts → GPU demand air-pocket → stress on circular/data-center debt → credit event. This chaining is why experts emphasize fragility over any single lever.

### Gaps
- No confirmed credit-event/default in AI data-center financing as of the sources found.

## Q2. Named institutional/bank warnings and their specific reasoning

### Takeaway
The IMF, Bank of England, BIS, and major bank strategists issued formal warnings in Oct 2025, centered on dotcom-level valuations plus extreme index concentration; their framing is "sharp correction is more likely," not an imminent systemic collapse.

### Cited Findings
- **IMF — Kristalina Georgieva** (speech ahead of IMF annual meetings, ~Oct 8 2025): told audiences to "buckle up"; said valuations "are heading toward levels we saw during the bullishness about the internet 25 years ago"; warned a sharp correction plus tighter financial conditions could drag down world growth — [Korea Times/AP](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/amp/world/20251009/is-there-an-ai-bubble-financial-institutions-sound-a-warning); [AI Magazine](https://aimagazine.com/news/buckle-up-the-imf-warns-that-ais-bubble-might-burst-soon)

- **Bank of England (Financial Policy Committee, Oct 2025):** risk of a "sharp correction" has grown; valuations "comparable to the peak" of the 2000 dotcom bubble; markets "particularly exposed should expectations around the impact of AI become less optimistic"; cited "disappointing AI capability/adoption progress or increased competition" as specific risks — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/security/alarms-bells-ring-as-bank-of-england-warns-of-sharp-market-correction-when-ai-bubble-bursts); [Colorado Biz](https://coloradobiz.com/ai-investment-bubble-warning-bank-of-england-imf/)
- **Jamie Dimon (JPMorgan):** grouped with IMF/BoE as warning of the damage from an AI-bubble burst (Oct 2025) — [naked capitalism headline, Oct 2025, page not directly fetchable](https://www.nakedcapitalism.com/2025/10/waiting-for-the-wheels-to-come-off-as-jamie-dimon-the-imf-and-bank-of-england-warn-of-damage-of-an-ai-bubble-burst.html)
- **Torsten Sløk (Apollo chief economist):** argues the AI bubble is *bigger than the 1990s* on valuation grounds — the top 10 S&P 500 companies have a higher forward P/E than the top 10 did in the mid-1990s; coverage in early 2024 and again July 2025 — [Fortune, Jul 17 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/07/17/ai-bubble-vs-dot-com-stocks-apollo-economist-torsten-slok/); [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/wall-streets-ai-bubble-is-worse-than-the-1999-dot-com-bubble-warns-a-top-economist-2000630487). ### Inferences

## Q3. Confidence/sentiment shock & figurehead (key-person) risk as a market catalyst

### Takeaway
A distinct and increasingly prominent thesis holds that because valuations price near-perfection and the index is historically concentrated, a confidence/sentiment shock can itself be the catalyst — and that Jensen Huang personally is a concentrated "key-man risk" whose departure or discrediting could trigger a sentiment reversal. This became a live story in Nov 2026 around a Huang scandal. This is the most directly relevant material for the downstream Huang-resignation scenario.

### Cited Findings — Reflexivity / confidence as its own trigger
- Soros-style reflexivity applied to AI: a two-way feedback loop where beliefs change fundamentals and vice-versa; circular deals blur real vs. manufactured demand — [Vincents](https://vincents.com.au/reflexivity-and-the-ai-boom-a-sorosian-analysis)
- Caballero (MIT working paper, "Speculative Growth and AI," June–July 2026): a price boom can create the fundamentals that later partly justify it; trigger is extrapolative valuation where investors treat perceived excess return as persistent; a "temporary belief wedge" raises investment before prices correct, and "the legacy survives only if the correction arrives late enough" — [MIT Economics PDF](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2026-07/speculative_growth_AI.pdf)
- "Because valuation already prices in near-perfection, even a small increase in perceived execution risk can produce a large price response" — [per WebSearch summary, Axios framing](https://www.axios.com/2026/11/09/jensen-huang-nvidia-ceo-crisis)

### Cited Findings — Jensen Huang as concentrated key-person risk
- Axios (Nov 9 2026): frames Huang as a "too big to fail" CEO; the AI economy's growth "disproportionately turns on" him; he has "repeatedly rescued the AI trade from bearish sentiment, including through backstops for companies such as CoreWeave and OpenAI"; flags the share-price test of whether board confidence holds — [Axios, Nov 9 2026 (summary; page not directly fetchable)](https://www.axios.com/2026/11/09/jensen-huang-nvidia-ceo-crisis)
- The Nov 2026 trigger event: fallout from Huang's admitted affair with a suspected Chinese spy; Nvidia's board said it had no reason to think national security/company secrets were compromised and backed his leadership; Trump called him "an honorable guy" — [Axios summary](https://www.axios.com/2026/11/09/jensen-huang-nvidia-ceo-crisis)
- Dan Ives (Wedbush), via CNN: "you can't replace him with one, two or three other candidates" — [per WebSearch summary](https://abc17news.com/?p=1663536)
- Analyst quote on succession: "Jensen is not replaceable... there is no other person on the management team bench that can step in seamlessly as CEO"; Nvidia runs a flat structure with 60+ executives reporting directly to Huang and has not disclosed a succession plan — [thevibes/Reuters-syndicated, Nov 2026](https://www.thevibes.com/articles/world/129364/nvidias-reliance-on-jensen-huang-exposes-ai-industry-to-leadership-risk)
- Michael Burry explicitly attributed much of the AI-bubble hype to "marketing efforts by Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang" — implying the narrative (and thus sentiment) is partly person-dependent — [Fortune, Nov 24 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/11/24/big-short-investor-michael-burry-nvidia-cisco-ai-bubble/); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/347888/big-short-guy-bet-1-1-billion-against-ai-giants-markets-still-absorbing)
- "Jensen Huang is living proof that AI's rockstar CEOs are also a hidden risk" — argues the boom leans on a handful of charismatic figureheads; absence of a publicly identified successor leaves a governance question unresolved — [abc17news/CNN syndication, Nov 2026](https://abc17news.com/?p=1663536); [ktvz](https://ktvz.com/?p=1663672)

### Inferences
- Experts generally rank *fundamental* triggers (ROI, rates, credit) above a pure confidence shock as the *root* cause, BUT the key-person/sentiment channel is widely treated as the *transmission mechanism* that could convert any wobble into a sharp correction, given concentration and near-perfection pricing.
- Huang is unusual among figureheads because he has acted as an active *backstop* (CoreWeave, OpenAI guarantees) — so his departure would remove not just a narrative anchor but a real financial circuit-breaker, plausibly making a Huang exit a stronger catalyst than a typical CEO departure. This is an inference the downstream scenario analysis should weigh; the Axios piece is the closest explicit support.

## Q5. Counterarguments — why the boom might be durable / resistant to a single shock

### Takeaway
Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan, BofA, and Huang himself argue this is more 1997 than 2000: earnings and cash flow (not debt) fund most capex, AI spend is still <1% of GDP, and circular deals are a small share of total spend — so the market should absorb isolated shocks.

### Cited Findings
- Goldman Sachs (Oct–Nov 2025): "not yet a bubble"; public-market valuations and capital-market activity remain below dotcom peaks; price gains have tracked profit growth; big tech's major investments paid for with free cash flow, not debt; AI spending <1% of US GDP vs 2–5% in prior large tech cycles; compares cycle to 1997–98, not 1999–2000 — [Benzinga, Oct 2025](https://benzinga.com/analyst-stock-ratings/analyst-color/25/10/48100265/fear-of-an-ai-bubble-this-time-is-different-goldman-says); [investinglive, Nov 10 2025](https://investinglive.com/stock-market-update/goldman-sachs-says-ai-boom-resembles-1997-tech-cycle-not-dotcom-peak-with-room-to-run-20251110/amp)
- Goldman concessions: some features "rhyme" with past bubbles; new IPOs average ~30% premiums (highest since dotcom); vendor financing among Meta/Alphabet/Amazon/Nvidia is a real parallel; private valuations raise red flags — [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/tech/25/11/48754164/is-ai-a-bubble-boom-rally-dot-com-analysis-goldman-sachs); [Wealth Professional](https://www.wealthprofessional.ca/news/industry-news/goldman-sachs-says-ai-not-yet-in-a-bubble-but-private-valuations-raise-red-flags/390697)
- BofA: circular deals are only ~5–10% of projected ~$5T AI spend by 2030 — i.e. too small to be systemic — [per WebSearch summary](https://benzinga.com/analyst-stock-ratings/analyst-color/25/10/48100265/fear-of-an-ai-bubble-this-time-is-different-goldman-says)
- Many Goldman and JPMorgan analysts "believe we are not in an AI-fueled bubble" — [WebSearch summary](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/one-ai-bubble-already-burst-112800354.html)
- Bull counterpoint: tech-sector profits still strong; no reckless behavior like a surge in risky IPOs — [itechguides](https://www.itechguides.com/what-could-cause-an-ai-investment-bubble-to-burst/)
- Jensen Huang (Nov 2025, BG2 podcast / Q3 earnings): rejects the bubble framing, calls it a "tipping point"; says ~a third of Nvidia's business (growing 30–40%/yr) does not depend on AI labs (a compute estimate, not a disclosed revenue split); "Nvidia can win in the absence of any AI labs" — [BNN Bloomberg, Nov 20 2025](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/business/artificial-intelligence/2025/11/20/tipping-point-or-bubble-nvidia-ceo-sees-ai-transformation-while-skeptics-count-the-risks/); [TIKR](https://www.tikr.com/blog/jensen-huang-declares-nvidia-can-win-in-the-absence-of-any-ai-labs)
- Durability-of-concentration counter-counterpoint: skeptics note Alphabet and Amazon designing their own chips; Steve Eisman flagged 70% of Nvidia accounts receivable from five customers; Seaport (sell rating): "The list of things that could go wrong for Nvidia is longer than the list of things that could go right" — [TIKR](https://www.tikr.com/blog/jensen-huang-declares-nvidia-can-win-in-the-absence-of-any-ai-labs); [CTV News](https://www.ctvnews.ca/sci-tech/article/tipping-point-or-bubble-nvidia-ceo-sees-ai-transformation-while-skeptics-count-the-risks/)

### Inferences
- The durability case rests chiefly on the claim that earnings/cash flow (not leverage) fund capex; if that claim erodes (rising hyperscaler debt, Oracle/neocloud leverage), the single strongest counterargument weakens — which is exactly the hinge the bears target.
- Even bulls concede valuation + concentration leave the market shock-sensitive; their argument is that the market can *absorb* shocks, not that shocks won't occur — consistent with the "sharp correction, not systemic collapse" institutional baseline.

### Gaps
- Sources don't reconcile Goldman's "funded by free cash flow" claim with the Oracle/neocloud debt figures in Q1; this tension is central and under-documented in what was retrieved.

## Q6. Recent (2025–2026) episodes that stress-tested AI-stock fragility

### Takeaway
Two clear stress tests — the Jan 2025 DeepSeek selloff and the Nov 2025 "good-earnings-still-sold" reversal — both showed the market is acutely sensitive to any challenge to the AI narrative, even absent a change in fundamentals; the Aug 2025 MIT-report wobble is a third, milder one.

### Cited Findings — DeepSeek selloff (Jan 27 2025)
