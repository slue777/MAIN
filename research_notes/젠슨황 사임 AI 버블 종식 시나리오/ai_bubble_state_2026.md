# State of the "AI Bubble" Debate as of Late 2026 (as of November 14, 2026)

> **Scope note for the report-writer:** This note characterizes AI valuations, capex, financing, and the bull/bear debate as of mid-November 2026. **Critical framing caveat:** the parent task treats Jensen Huang's resignation as a *hypothetical trigger* for a scenario analysis, but in the live news record as of this research date, **Huang has already resigned (November 12, 2026) amid a China-intelligence/affair investigation, and NVIDIA's stock has already fallen ~31% from its October 6, 2026 high.** Findings below therefore reflect a market *already* mid-wobble. I flag which figures are pre-shock peaks vs. post-shock. All dollar figures USD. Many figures come from a late-2026 news environment that appears internally consistent across Reuters/CNBC/WSJ/Bloomberg/FT/IMF/BIS; some aggregator and blog sources are used where they quote primary data, and are labeled.

## Key Question 1: Valuations, market caps of leading AI names, and index concentration

### Takeaway
As of late October 2026 the S&P 500 was at record highs (7,818.93 on Oct 6) with the Magnificent 7 at ~34-35% of the index and a combined value above $23-24 trillion — the most concentrated the index has ever been. NVIDIA peaked near a $5.16T market cap, then lost ~$1.7-1.8T (roughly 31%) into mid-November 2026 after the Huang scandal, falling below $4T and behind Apple and Microsoft. Private leaders OpenAI (~$1.25T) and Anthropic (IPO targeting ~$1.3T+) reached valuations that themselves triggered VC fears of a correction.

- OpenAI's failure to hit its $30B/$1.4T target and the tempering of Anthropic's IPO expectations are the first concrete signs that even private-market AI valuations hit a ceiling in late 2026.

## Key Question 2: Scale of AI capex and forward projections; signs of overcapacity/demand softening

### Takeaway
2026 is the largest capex spree in history: the big US hyperscalers are on track for roughly $800B+ in capex this year (Goldman: +94% YoY), scaling toward $1.2T (2027) and $1.4T (2028); broader AI-infra spending forecasts run to $2.6T/year by 2030 and multi-trillion cumulative totals. Multiple institutions (Gartner, Bain, IMF) now warn capex is outrunning plausible AI revenue, with Gartner flagging up to $3.5T in potential write-downs.

### Cited Findings
- **Goldman Sachs: hyperscaler capex ≈ $800B in 2026 (+94% from ~$412B in 2025), rising to $1.2T in 2027 and $1.4T in 2028** — [Goldman via RIA/Lance Roberts](https://realinvestmentadvice.com/resources/blog/ai-capex-and-earnings-what-happens-when-growth-slows/), citing Ben Snider, Goldman Sachs Research, Sept 23, 2026. FactSet's July tally also ≈$800B for CY2026.
- **UBS raised aggregate AI capex forecasts to $1.0T (2026), $1.4T (2027), $1.5T (2028)** — [UBS CIO, Nov 2, 2026](https://www.ubs.com/global/en/wealthmanagement/insights/chief-investment-office/house-view/daily/2026/latest-02112026.html).
- **Five hyperscalers spent $556.2B in the first three quarters of 2026; full-year 2026 projected $803B-$828B** (broad capex, not only AI) — [Business Insider, Oct 2026](https://www.businessinsider.com/amazon-alphabet-meta-microsoft-spacex-capex-ai-spending-total-2026-10). Within this: **Amazon ~$220B, Alphabet ~$200B (raised), Microsoft ~$170B (trimmed from higher), Meta ~$130-145B, Oracle $50-70B** — BI and [Software Strategies Blog](https://softwarestrategiesblog.com/2026/10/22/ai-infrastructure-spending-forecasts-compared...).
- **Record future commitments: hyperscalers have committed ~$2.6 trillion in future spending across data-center leases and buildout** — [Reddit/r/investing summary of guides](https://www.reddit.com/r/investing/comments/1xeraf9/...) (secondary; corroborated directionally by Morgan Stanley below).
- **BCG: AI infrastructure will need ~$2.6 trillion in *annual* spending by 2030**; a gigawatt of 2026 compute must earn **~$15B/GW to cover costs** — [BCG, 2026](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/who-pays-for-ai-infrastructure).
- **Morgan Stanley: ~$2.9 trillion in data-center capex through 2028**, funded ~$1.4T from hyperscaler cash flow + ~$800B private credit; separately a ~**$1.81T FY2028** hyperscaler capex figure cited — [Software Strategies Blog](https://softwarestrategiesblog.com/2026/10/22/...); [Moomoo/MS](https://www.facebook.com/moomoosingapore/posts/...).
- **Gartner: data-center systems spending $879B in 2026 (+73.8%), $1.37T in 2027 (+55.5%)** — [Gartner IT forecast, Oct 21, 2026](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-10-21-...), via [AI Business Weekly](https://aibusinessweekly.net/p/ai-spending-statistics).
- **Overcapacity / return-gap warnings:** Gartner warns the capex trajectory is "**unsustainable**" with a **~$3.5 trillion write-down risk**, and that total AI revenue attributable to AI data centers will reach **no more than ~$3.6 trillion** — [Fierce Network](https://www.fierce-network.com/cloud/gartner-warns-ai-data-center-capex-unsustainable-35t-write-down-risk-looms); [CIO.com](https://www.cio.com/article/4236629/...) (Gartner + Bain: capex will "far exceed forecast revenue through 2030").
- **Demand-side softening signals:** Microsoft trimmed CY capex outlook to ~$170B citing third-party data-center delays; Tesla cut 2026 capex by >$5B — [BI](https://www.businessinsider.com/microsoft-trims-ai-spending-plans...); [Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/tesla-isn-t-hyperscaler-why-205302055.html). McKinsey: ~$7T on data centers by 2030 (>$5T AI-specific) — [programs.com/McKinsey](https://programs.com/resources/data-center-statistics/).

### Inferences
- The forecasts cluster around ~$800B-$1.0T for 2026 and ~$1.2-1.4T for 2027, so the "scale" numbers are robust; the *disputed* part is forward demand/ROI, where Gartner/Bain/IMF explicitly model capex exceeding monetizable revenue.
- Microsoft's trim and Tesla's cut are the first concrete data-points cited as possible demand/supply-chain softening, though bulls (below) read Microsoft's as a supply (capacity-delay) issue, not demand.

### Gaps
- No company has given concrete 2027 capex guidance yet ("all signs point upward" per BI); forward numbers are analyst estimates, not disclosures.

## Key Question 3: Circular financing, vendor financing, SPVs, special-purpose debt — specific deals and amounts

### Takeaway
"Circular financing" — where the same firms are simultaneously investor, supplier, and customer to each other — is now a central systemic concern, flagged by the IMF (14 circular arrangements) and BIS. Documented structures include NVIDIA's up-to-$100B+ OpenAI investment and $500B third-party-capital platform, Broadcom-arranged private credit for OpenAI/Anthropic, SpaceX's $40B GPU-financing package, and Oracle's off-balance-sheet chip-leasing SPV. AI-related debt issuance has surged past ~$466-489B in 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **IMF October 2026 GFSR flagged 14 circular financing arrangements across the GPU ecosystem** and warned of a correction cascading through a "**highly leveraged and circular AI ecosystem**" — [I/O Fund](https://io-fund.com/ai-stocks/ai-debt-nears-500-billion-nvidia-oracle-coreweave); [IMF/Georgieva](https://www.imf.org/en/blogs/articles/2026/10/15/the-world-cant-afford-to-get-ai-wrong) via [GARP](https://www.garp.org/risk-intelligence/technology/will-ai-cause-financial-crisis-261113). The IMF has an explicit technical note defining circular financing (Chen, Gautam, Wu) — [IMF note](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/tnm/2026/english/tnmea2026013.pdf).
- **BIS Bulletin (Oct 1, 2026; Jon Frost et al.): of all AI-to-AI investment deals 2021-2025, 16.1% by deal count and 46.4% by deal value also involved commercial supply-chain relationships** between investor and target — [BIS Bulletin 137](https://www.bis.org/publications/bulletin-137-circular-relationships-among-ai-firms) via GARP.
- **NVIDIA → OpenAI:** NVIDIA investing up to **$100B** in OpenAI (giving OpenAI money that returns to NVIDIA via chip purchases); earlier reporting cited a **$30B** direct investment — [Instagram/IMF summary](https://www.instagram.com/reel/Dfs_bGqzEvn/); [Benzinga](https://www.facebook.com/Benzinga/posts/...); [Morningstar via Harvard PON](https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/dealmaking-daily/what-are-circular-deals/). Jay Goldberg: "like having your parents co-sign your first mortgage" — [Bloomberg, Sept 2025](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-09-23/nvidia-s-massive-openai-deal-fuels-circular-financing-concerns).
- **NVIDIA $500B third-party-capital platform:** NVIDIA signed preliminary agreements with six institutional partners — **Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs, KKR** — targeting **>$500B in third-party capital**, with Huang stating NVIDIA retains the option to **backstop up to $125B** across these structures — [I/O Fund](https://io-fund.com/ai-stocks/ai-debt-nears-500-billion-nvidia-oracle-coreweave). Michael Burry criticized the "$500 billion Nvidia Wall Street" structure (25% stakes + residual-value guarantees on chip purchases) and is short NVDA — [US News](https://money.usnews.com/investing/articles/will-circular-financing-pop-the-ai-bubble).
- **SpaceX–NVIDIA:** reported **$40B package = $10B bank loans + $30B investment-grade bonds (led by Apollo)** to buy NVIDIA GPUs (direct-borrowing model) — [I/O Fund](https://io-fund.com/ai-stocks/ai-debt-nears-500-billion-nvidia-oracle-coreweave); [WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/tech/oracle-broadcom-and-spacex-seek-blockbuster-debt-deals...).
- **Broadcom–OpenAI / Broadcom–Anthropic:** Broadcom arranging **>$50B in private credit** (Apollo, Blackstone) so OpenAI can finance custom silicon; mirrors an earlier Anthropic deal where **Broadcom guarantees up to $42B in senior notes** (payable only on Anthropic default if chip resale value falls below a set level) — [I/O Fund](https://io-fund.com/ai-stocks/ai-debt-nears-500-billion-nvidia-oracle-coreweave); [Reuters, Oct 1, 2026](https://www.reuters.com/business/broadcom-lend-anthropic-up-42-billion-lease-its-chips-filing-says-2026-10-01/).
- **Oracle SPV / off-balance-sheet leasing:** Oracle in talks with **Apollo and Goldman Sachs** to set up a separate investor-funded entity to buy chips and lease them to Oracle (keeps debt off Oracle's balance sheet). Oracle disclosed **$288B of new data-center leases not yet commenced, terms 15-19 years** — [I/O Fund](https://io-fund.com/ai-stocks/ai-debt-nears-500-billion-nvidia-oracle-coreweave); [Oracle 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1341439/000119312526389274/orcl-20260831.htm).
- **AI-related debt issuance 2026:** Morgan Stanley — **$466B through September 2026** (vs $101B same period 2025, >4x); Goldman — **$489B by July 22, 2026** (vs $322B for all of 2025), hyperscalers ~40% ($194B). Monthly issuance peaked at **$113B in June**, slowed to **$23B in September** — [I/O Fund](https://io-fund.com/ai-stocks/ai-debt-nears-500-billion-nvidia-oracle-coreweave).
- **IMF: five tech firms (Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Microsoft, Oracle) accounted for ~10% of all global investment-grade corporate bond issuance in 2026, up from ~0.5% in 2024** — [IMF GFSR Oct 2026 via I/O Fund](https://io-fund.com/ai-stocks/ai-debt-nears-500-billion-nvidia-oracle-coreweave).
- **Debt as share of capex:** Goldman estimates debt funds ~1/3 of hyperscaler capex (2/3 from operating cash flow), and that hyperscalers could borrow **~$2T more** while keeping IG ratings — [I/O Fund](https://io-fund.com/ai-stocks/ai-debt-nears-500-billion-nvidia-oracle-coreweave).
- **S&P Global warns top hyperscalers likely have MORE AI-linked (off-balance-sheet) debt than currently known** — [Reuters, Oct 19, 2026](https://www.reuters.com/business/top-hyperscalers-likely-have-more-ai-linked-debt-than-now-known-sp-global-warns-2026-10-19/).
- **BIS "Building AI on credit" (Drehmann & Eren):** much hyperscaler and project-vehicle debt is long-dated and placed with NBFIs that also hold Treasuries and IG corporates; spillovers to Treasury/IG markets "small and short-lived" so far but "sustained issuance at the current pace could change this" — [BIS Bulletin 140](https://www.bis.org/publications/bulletin-140-building-ai-credit--structures-and-spillovers) via GARP.
- **US Treasury TBAC (Nov 3, 2026):** competition for capital "appears contained within the tech credit ecosystem"; no clear evidence SI-related issuance is crowding out Treasuries; 10-yr yield rise attributed primarily to capex-driven growth + energy shocks — [TBAC minutes](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0680/) via GARP.

### Inferences
- The circular structures concentrate credit risk back onto the chip vendors (NVIDIA backstops, Broadcom guarantees), so a demand shortfall could transmit from AI labs → neoclouds → chipmakers rather than being diffused — the opposite of the dispersed 2008 subprime structure (per Dudley, below).
- The September collapse in monthly debt issuance ($113B → $23B) is itself an early-warning signal: markets began rationing AI credit before the November equity wobble.

### Gaps
- Off-balance-sheet magnitude is explicitly unknowable (S&P Global, IMF both say disclosure is incomplete) — a key uncertainty for any scenario model.

## Key Question 4: Prominent voices — bears vs. bulls

### Takeaway
By November 2026 the bear chorus is dominated by official institutions (IMF, BIS, BoE/FSB, ECB, Fed officials, Treasury-secretary alumni) plus economists and short-sellers (Burry, Dalio, Lachman, Bernstein); the bull case rests on earnings reality (record profits, ~19x forward P/E) and is argued by Morgan Stanley, Fundstrat's Tom Lee, Wedbush's Dan Ives, David Sacks, and — with hedging — Sam Altman.

### Inferences
- The notable feature of the late-2026 debate is that the *warners are now the institutions* (IMF, BIS, BoE, ECB, Fed, Treasury alumni), while the *defenders are sell-side and operators* — a reversal from early-cycle dynamics and a classic late-bubble signature.
- Even bulls (Ives, Altman) concede parts are bubbly; the bull case is narrowly about hyperscaler earnings durability, not about neoclouds or startups.

### Gaps
- Didn't capture a detailed quantified Goldman/Morgan Stanley "fair value" counter-model beyond the 19x P/E point.

## Key Question 5: Key bear metrics (revenue-vs-capex gaps, depreciation, ROI, power)

### Takeaway
Bears point to five quantified pressure points: (1) ~half of 2026 S&P 500 earnings growth is itself the capex cycle, so earnings fall when spending merely stops accelerating; (2) depreciation on a 5-6-year schedule turns today's "free" capex into future expense; (3) ROI evidence is weak (MIT: ~95% of enterprise GenAI pilots show no measurable P&L); (4) revenue needed to justify capex far exceeds plausible AI revenue (Gartner, BCG); (5) hyperscaler free cash flow is turning negative amid rising rates.

### Cited Findings
- **Capex IS the earnings:** Goldman estimates **~half of 2026 S&P 500 earnings growth traces to AI capex**; a **$250B capex surprise moves S&P 500 EPS growth ~6 percentage points**; if 2027 capex merely matched 2026's record (flat at $800B), implied EPS growth falls to **~1%**. FactSet 2026 S&P 500 EPS growth **32.8%, IT sector 65%** — [RIA/Goldman/FactSet](https://realinvestmentadvice.com/resources/blog/ai-capex-and-earnings-what-happens-when-growth-slows/).
- **Depreciation "golden window":** sellers book revenue immediately; buyers capitalize and expense over **5-6 years**, so the economy records supplier profit before buyer expense — the catch arrives "when the spending stops growing," not when it stops — [RIA](https://realinvestmentadvice.com/resources/blog/ai-capex-and-earnings-what-happens-when-growth-slows/).
- **Mark-to-market circularity in earnings:** "Large technology companies recorded roughly **$150B of unrealized investment gains in private companies in Q2 2026, ≈12% of S&P 500 EPS**" (Ben Snider, Goldman) — marks that can reverse — [RIA](https://realinvestmentadvice.com/resources/blog/ai-capex-and-earnings-what-happens-when-growth-slows/).
- **Chip margins:** semiconductor gross margins running **~80%, roughly double historical average**; Goldman expects this to fade — [RIA](https://realinvestmentadvice.com/resources/blog/ai-capex-and-earnings-what-happens-when-growth-slows/).
- **Free cash flow deterioration:** FactSet expects FCF ≈ zero or negative in FY2026 for every hyperscaler **except Alphabet and Microsoft**; the group carries ~**$700B debt + ~$820B lease commitments**. Alphabet posted its **first negative FCF quarter since its 2004 IPO in Q2 2026 (−$5.9B)** as quarterly capex hit $44.9B (TTM FCF still +$53.3B) — [RIA/FactSet](https://realinvestmentadvice.com/...); [I/O Fund](https://io-fund.com/ai-stocks/ai-debt-nears-500-billion-nvidia-oracle-coreweave).
- **ROI evidence weak:** MIT (Project NANDA, "GenAI Divide," Aug 2025): **~95% of enterprise GenAI pilots showed no measurable P&L impact** (only ~5% achieved rapid value). *Heavily caveated:* it describes organizations, not a count of failed pilots; preliminary/not peer-reviewed; partly reflects missing baselines — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo/); [CloudZero](https://www.cloudzero.com/blog/enterprise-ai-roi/); [AI-First claim-check](https://www.ai-first.company/evidence/claim-checks/mit-95-percent-of-ai-pilots-fail/). Related: S&P Global found **42% abandoned most AI projects in 2025**; IBM put the share hitting expected ROI near **25%** — [TechJournal](https://techjournal.org/ai-spending-reckoning-2026).
- **Revenue-vs-capex gap:** Gartner — AI revenue attributable to AI data centers reaches **no more than ~$3.6T**, against capex Gartner calls "unsustainable" (~$3.5T write-down risk); BCG — a 2026 gigawatt of compute must earn **~$15B/GW** to cover costs — [Fierce](https://www.fierce-network.com/cloud/gartner-warns-ai-data-center-capex-unsustainable-35t-write-down-risk-looms); [BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/who-pays-for-ai-infrastructure).
- **Rates / financing stress:** Fed hiked Sept 16, 2026 to **3.75-4.00%** (first increase since 2023), Warsh: "inflation is too high"; **10-yr Treasury yield 5.10% (Oct 15), touched 5.24% (Oct 9) and 5.35% (Oct 7)** — [RIA](https://realinvestmentadvice.com/...); [I/O Fund](https://io-fund.com/ai-stocks/ai-debt-nears-500-billion-nvidia-oracle-coreweave).
- **Specific balance-sheet stress (neoclouds/Oracle):** Oracle — $125.3B total borrowings vs $37.1B cash; interest expense $1.43B (+55% YoY), >7% of revenue; FY2026 FCF **−$23.7B**. CoreWeave — $35.6B total debt; Q2 rev $2.6B vs capex $9.4B; interest $640M (+140% YoY); **>25% of revenue goes to debt service** — [I/O Fund](https://io-fund.com/ai-stocks/ai-debt-nears-500-billion-nvidia-oracle-coreweave); [RIA on Oracle](https://realinvestmentadvice.com/resources/blog/oracle-sells-cheap-stock-as-its-debt-window-narrows/).
- **Historical template:** US communications-equipment investment rose from ~$62B (1996) to >$135B (Q4 2000), then fell ~31% within a year — "the fiber was real... shareholders still got wiped out" — [Richmond Fed via RIA](https://realinvestmentadvice.com/...).
- **Power/regulation constraint:** AI data-center moratorium bills filed in ≥9 state legislatures in 2026; federal AI Data Center Moratorium Act (S.4214) stalled in committee; no statewide moratorium law yet, but any delay pushes projects out a year+ — [RIA](https://realinvestmentadvice.com/...). Hyperscaler emissions expected +60% in 2026 (proxy for power demand) — [Trellis](https://trellis.net/article/hyperscaler-emissions-2026-bricola-group/).

### Inferences
- The "second-derivative" argument (earnings crack when capex *growth* slows, not when capex falls) is the single most important bear metric for a trigger-event scenario: even a flat-but-high 2027 capex implies ~1% S&P 500 EPS growth, so a confidence shock that freezes incremental spending is sufficient to end the earnings boom without any absolute capex decline.
- The combination of negative hyperscaler FCF + rising rates + the Sept debt-issuance slowdown means the financing channel is already tightening — bears' "financing cracks before demand" thesis.

### Gaps
- No single clean figure for aggregate AI-infrastructure *revenue* in 2026 to directly net against the ~$800B capex (Gartner's $3.6T is a cumulative/forward ceiling, not a 2026 actual).

Breadth was already poor beneath record index highs.
